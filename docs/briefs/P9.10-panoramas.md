# P9.10 360° images

**Work package:** P9.10. It needs P9.9 (PR #78).

**Plan line:** 360° images with hotspots and markers (guided or free exploration).

**My design choices** (Luke asked to go through all of Phase 9), listed for him to change:

1. **A new object type, `panorama`:** one equirectangular picture (twice as wide as it is high, the usual form a 360° camera saves), seen through the object's frame as a window.
   - The learner looks around by dragging, or with the arrow keys when it has focus.
   - Zoom with the mouse wheel, or + and −.
2. **Spots** are points on the picture, given by their direction:
   - yaw from −180° to 180°;
   - pitch from −90° to 90°.

   A spot is a **hotspot** (a button that only fires triggers) or a **marker** (a button whose label shows a title and plain text, as P9.1's markers do).

3. **Guided or free:**
   - **Free:** the learner looks wherever they like and opens spots in any order.
   - **Guided:** Previous and Next buttons turn the view to each spot in the author's order and open it. The learner can still look around.
4. **Trigger events:**
   - "a 360° spot is clicked" (object, spot);
   - "all spots in a 360° image are visited" (object).

   Spots visited are kept with the slide, as other progress is, and resumed.

5. **Drawing:**
   - **In a course:** a WebGL view of the sphere, with the spots as real buttons placed over it, so the keyboard and screen readers reach them. They are listed in order, named by their names.
   - **Without WebGL:** the picture pans flat (yaw moves it across), and the spots are still buttons.
   - **In the editor and thumbnails:** the whole picture flat, with each spot drawn as a numbered dot where it is. That doubles as the map the author places spots on.
6. **Editor:**
   - Insert → **360° Image…** picks a picture (any of the usual picture types; a 2:1 picture works best).
   - The Format panel's **360° image** section:
     - free or guided;
     - where the view starts (yaw, pitch, zoom);
     - the spots, each with name, kind, yaw, pitch and, for markers, a title and text;
     - add, remove, and up and down for the guided order.
   - **Double-clicking the flat picture** on the stage adds a spot at that point.

## Saved format version 34

- **A new object type `panorama`**, whose props are:
  - `asset`;
  - `mode` (`free` or `guided`);
  - `start { yaw, pitch, fov }` (fov from 30° to 100°);
  - `spots: { id, name, kind, yaw, pitch, title, body }[]` (at most 50).
- **Trigger events:**
  - `{ type: 'spotClicked', object, spot }`;
  - `{ type: 'panoramaVisited', object }`.
- **Validation:**
  - the asset is a picture;
  - spot ids are unique in the project, and triggers name the object's own spots;
  - removing a spot removes the triggers that wait for it.
- **Migration 33 → 34:** nothing changes. Legacy fixture `v33-two-slides`.

## Done when

  - schema and validation;
  - removing a spot and its triggers;
  - migration and fixture;
  - the property test adds panoramas and spots.
- **Engine:**
  - a spot click fires its trigger and marks it visited;
  - "all visited" fires once;
  - visited spots resume.
- **Render:**
  - the flat editor drawing with numbered spots;
  - the course's container, with spot buttons and their names;
  - a visual reference.
- **Player:**
  - the projection maths (unit tests: a spot straight ahead is at the middle, one behind is hidden, the edges);
  - browser (Chromium and Firefox): dragging turns the view, keys turn it, clicking a spot runs its trigger, a marker's label opens, and guided Next turns to and opens the next spot.
- **e2e:**
  - insert a 360° image;
  - add two spots;
  - a trigger on one;
  - guided mode;
  - save and reopen;
  - publish, and use it.
