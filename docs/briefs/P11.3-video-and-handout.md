# P11.3 Additional publish targets: handout and video

**Plan:** "Video (mp4 render of a linear run-through), printable handout (PDF/Word of slides and notes)."

Two things a course can become besides a course:

- a handout, to print or send, of each slide and its notes;
- a video of the course played straight through.

Neither is a publish target in the saved settings: each is its own command on the File menu, with its own small dialog. So there is no saved-format change.

The package is built in three parts, each on its own branch. The plan's box is ticked after part C.

## Part A: the handout as PDF (`wp/p11.3a-handout-pdf`)

**File → Export Handout…** opens a dialog:

- **Layout:**
  - one slide a page with its notes below (the default);
  - or three slides a page with lines beside each for the reader's own notes.
- **Which slides:**
  - all;
  - or the current scene.

  Lightbox-only slides and question banks are left out.

- **Title:** the course's title at the top of the first page, and page numbers in the footer.

**What a slide looks like:**

- each slide as it first appears, on its layout and master, drawn by the renderer the stage uses (`renderSlide`), without animation;
- the slide's number and title above it;
- notes as written (rich text).

**How:**

- The renderer builds the handout inside the editor's own page, in an element shown only when printing (`@media print`), so it has the editor's fonts, styles and media.
- Main then prints the window to PDF (`webContents.printToPDF`) and saves the file where the author says.
- No other window is opened, and nothing leaves the machine.

**Tests:**

- unit: which slides are included and in what order (scenes, and lightbox and bank slides left out);
- e2e: export a two-slide project's handout. The PDF must exist, begin `%PDF`, and hold the slide titles and notes text.

**As built (session 94):**

- `handoutSlides` and `handoutPages` (`renderer/handout.ts`) pick and page the slides. Slides are numbered through the whole course, even when one scene is chosen.
- The handout is drawn while the dialog is open, as `.print-handout`.
  - On screen it is `display: none`.
  - When printed, everything else is `visibility: hidden`, since the handout sits deep in the editor's tree. The page background is white.
- The slide pictures are the renderer's own HTML (`renderSlide`), scaled with a transform; the export waits for their images to load. Notes are `renderRichText`.
- Main prints to A4 with half-inch margins, page numbers in the footer, and a tagged PDF (`generateTaggedPDF`), so the PDF has a structure screen readers can use.
- `%Variable%` references show as written, as they do in the editor.

## Part B: the handout as Word (`wp/p11.3b-handout-word`)

The same choices, written as a `.docx`, with each slide as a picture above its notes:

- pictures of slides: the handout element is laid out off-screen in the editor's page, and main captures each slide (`capturePage` of its bounds) at twice its size;
- the document: our own minimal `.docx` writer (P10.3 part B made one for translation documents), with the pictures, headings and the notes' paragraphs.

Tests: the document's parts and pictures, and that Word-compatible readers accept it (`yauzl` reads it back; the XML parses).

**As built (session 95):**

- The dialog's **Export Word…** sends each slide's markup (`toMarkup(renderSlide(...))`), number, title and notes to main. The notes are plain paragraphs: bullets start "• ", numbered items their number.
- **Pictures** (`pictureSlides`):
  - each slide's markup is written to a temporary page with the editor's rich-text styles, and loaded in one hidden, offscreen window with scripts off;
  - it is captured, and its media load through the app's media scheme.
  - A window cannot be larger than the screen (here it was held to 1440 × 852), so each slide is drawn as large as fits, up to twice its size, keeping its shape.
- **The document** (`handout-document.ts`):
  - title and heading styles, so it has an outline;
  - each picture inline with a description for screen readers;
  - notes as paragraphs;
  - a page each, or a table of picture and lines with a page after every third.
- **Checked by eye:**
  - the pictures, which hold the slide's own picture;
  - the full LibreOffice Writer opening the document and turning it into a PDF of two pages, with title, headings and framed pictures.

## Part C: the video (`wp/p11.3c-video`)

**Luke's decision (2026-11-22, session 98): WebM only** (VP9 video, Opus sound), in keeping with P6.3's royalty-free formats. The three choices offered were:

- WebM only;
- MP4 with H.264 and AAC;
- MP4 through the system's own encoders.

**File → Export Video…** makes a video of the course played straight through:

- **Order:** each slide in course order (as the handout: scenes' slides, without lightbox-only slides or banks), for the length of its timeline, then the next. Branches, questions and waits for clicks are not taken.
- **Picture:** the slide as the learner sees it, drawn by the real player (as Preview is), at the stage's size and 30 frames a second. Animations, transitions and motion paths play.
- **Sound:** each sound object that plays with its timeline, mixed in at its place on its slide, at its volume.
- **Progress** is shown, and the export can be cancelled. The file is written only when complete.
- **The dialog** shows the video's length and size before exporting.
- **Without ffmpeg** (a missing `vendor/ffmpeg`; macOS has its own build since session 103), it says it cannot make a video here.

**How:**

- **Playing:** the course is built as for Preview (`publishWeb`), with a page that starts the player without its own frame loop (`autoStart: false`) and with a clock main sets. It is served under the preview scheme and played in a hidden, offscreen window that is never shown.
- **Frame by frame:** for each frame, main sets the clock, ticks the player, waits for it to paint, and captures the stage. At a slide's end it jumps to the next (`engine.jumpToSlide`). A slow machine makes the export slower; the video is the same.
- **Encoding:** the frames go to ffmpeg as raw pictures through a pipe, with the sounds as separate inputs placed by `adelay` and mixed by `amix`. The output is VP9 and Opus in WebM.
- **What is not in the video:** the sound of video objects (only their picture), a course's web objects from an address (as in Preview), and anything a learner would click.

**Tests:**

- unit: the schedule (order, lengths, frame counts), the ffmpeg arguments and the sound mix;
- e2e (where ffmpeg is fetched): a two-slide course exported to a WebM whose ffprobe shows VP9 at the stage's size, the expected length, and Opus when the course has a sound.

**As built (session 99):**

- **The plan** (`video/plan.ts`, pure, tested): `videoSchedule`, `videoSounds` and `ffmpegArguments`.
  - Each slide shows for at least half a second, in whole frames at 30 a second.
  - A sound that a trigger plays is left out, and one that runs past its slide's end is cut there.
- **The export** (`video/export.ts`):
  - Previews now serves more than one course (`host`, `unhost`), so the video's course has a token of its own in the preview session.
  - Its page sets a clock main controls, starts the player with `autoStart: false`, and makes `HTMLMediaElement.prototype.play` do nothing.
    - Sound that played would report its own time and move timelines in real time, and could be heard.
    - The sound is mixed in by ffmpeg instead, and the window is muted as well.
  - For each frame: set the clock, `jumpToSlide` at a slide's first frame, `tick`, wait two animation frames, capture the stage, and write its BGRA bitmap to ffmpeg's input.
  - The stage's box is rounded inwards, so no edge of the player's frame is caught.
  - ffmpeg scales to the stage's size (made even) and writes VP9 (CRF 32) and Opus to `<name>.webm.part`, renamed when complete.
  - Cancelled or failed, the part file is removed.
- **Video objects** show their first frame or poster, not their playing picture, and their sound is not in the video; noted in the dialog's words for a run-through.
- **Checked:**
  - frames at 1 s and 2.8 s by eye, the first slide's shape and then the second slide;
  - the sound by level: silent on the first slide, the tone on the second;
  - the e2e reads pixels at those moments.
- **Speed:** a 3.5-second course takes about 5 seconds to make on this machine.
