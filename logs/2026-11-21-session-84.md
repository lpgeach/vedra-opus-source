## What was done

- **`isRightToLeft(tag)`** in the model. It knows Arabic, Hebrew (and the old `iw`), Persian, Urdu, Pashto, Sindhi, Uyghur, Yiddish, Central Kurdish, Divehi, Kashmiri, Syriac, Aramaic and Khowar, with any region.
  - A script subtag decides when there is one: `az-Arab` is right to left, `ur-Latn` is not.
  - Hausa is left out on purpose: it is mostly written in Latin script today.
- **The player frame** takes `dir="rtl"` for a right-to-left course (in `applyLook`). The footer, the bar, the tabs, the menu and the text view run right to left.
  - The slide is not mirrored: its objects stay where the author put them.
- **Every paragraph and list item** is drawn with `dir="auto"` (render). Arabic words in an English course run right to left, and English words in an Arabic one left to right, each aligned to its own start.
  - In the editor, text being edited does the same (`unicode-bidi: plaintext`).
- **The Player dialog's "Words for"** chooser appears when more than one language has words. It shows and edits the words kept for each language, from a translation or typed in before the language changed, without changing the course's language.

## Tests

- **Model** (`rtl.test.ts`): languages, regions, case, scripts both ways, and languages that are not right to left.
- **Render:** paragraphs and list items carry `dir="auto"`.
- **Player unit:** `applyLook` sets `dir`.
- **Player browser** (`rtl.spec.ts`), in each browser:
  - an Arabic course's frame is `rtl`, with Previous on the right;
  - its Arabic paragraph is `rtl` and its English one `ltr`;
  - the slide's objects are not moved;
  - an English course is `ltr`, with an Arabic paragraph still `rtl`.
- **e2e** (`player-words.spec.ts`):
  - French words, then the course made Arabic: the French are kept;
  - the chooser lists both;
  - the French are edited while the course stays Arabic;
  - saved, reopened, and still there.
